# Living NPCs — Final Spec (Revision 3)

A modular, game-agnostic simulation core for lazy NPC generation, progressive
discovery, finite populations, relationship simulation, and constrained
dialogue rendering.

---

## 1) The Idea

The world implies millions of NPCs; none exist until observed. Facts about an
NPC live as weighted possibilities defined in JSON; observation collapses
them into permanent canon. Observation is any act of attention — talking,
watching someone fight, pulling a behavior profile for the combat AI.
Authoring effort goes into trait pools and conditional rules ("200 NPCs of
work"); combinatorics plus coherence rules yield millions of plausible,
persistent, unique individuals. Cost — storage, compute, inference — scales
with what has been observed, never with the size of the implied world.

The engine's defining law: **the world is frozen except where attention
touches it — and no one can prove otherwise**, because the only way to check
is to observe, and observing is what makes it real.

---

## 2) The Four Spaces

**Character space (canon).** Facts: gender, age band, profession, family,
and trait-class facts (bravery, strength, skills) expressed as 1–10 enums.
Uncollapsed = weighted distribution; collapsed = permanent, never rerolled.

**Speech space (the collapse trigger).** Dialogue creates canon rather than
reading it. An NPC may only say what is already collapsed or what collapses
in that utterance — the blacksmith mentioning his starving children IS the
collapse of `has_children: yes`. Self-contradiction is structurally
impossible.

**Role/population space (the finite urn).** A place's demographics are a
census, not independent dice. Discovered artists deplete the artist pool;
unique roles (the mayor) bind to one `entity_id` forever — street encounter
and office visit resolve to the same person. Above a population threshold,
depletion bookkeeping is skipped (finite population correction (1 − n/N)
makes the error provably negligible — 50 met of 100,000 is a 0.05% skew).

**Observer/relationship space (canon vs. knowledge).** World truth and
player knowledge are separate layers. `collapsed_facts` belong to the
entity — global, rolled once. `known_facts` and the intimacy score belong to
an (entity, observer) record. A second observer never rerolls; they just
haven't read pages already written. NPCs keep met-lists of observers,
enabling "have you met my friend?" and "you two know each other — small
world." Useful in single-player (UI, journals, gating); makes co-op possible
later at zero redesign cost. Multiplayer is not a design goal.

## 3) The Mathematics

**Model: a Bayesian network, sampled lazily.** Facts are nodes; dependencies
(profession→gender, age→has_children, profession→skills) are edges; each
fact needs only a conditional table over its parents, not the full joint.
The `collapse_order` is the topological order of the DAG. Collapse is
ancestral (forward) sampling — the textbook algorithm, which the V1 design
implemented by accident.

**The lean theorem: nothing is ever updated.** Live probability maintenance
is only needed for *uncollapsed* facts — which have no state. A fact's
distribution is computed exactly once, at collapse, conditioned on
already-canon parents. Between collapses the machinery costs zero.

**Resolution formula.** final_weights = base_weights × ∏(active rule
factors), elementwise, clamp negatives, normalize. Rules are factors:
`set_weights` (row replacement), `multiply_weights` (likelihood factor),
`add_weights` (additive prior). Multi-parent facts (has_children ←
age_band + marital_status) are just multiple factors on one vector.
Fully collapsing a 25-fact NPC ≈ a few hundred multiplications —
sub-microsecond. A thousand NPCs could collapse in a frame.

**Direction discipline.** The DAG forbids cycles: each dependency pair gets
ONE authored direction (the collapse_order forces it); the validator rejects
rules pointing against the order. Reverse inference (saw her in the market →
profession odds) is Bayes' theorem on the same tables — trivial at this
scale, but out of scope until needed.

**Collapse order ≠ discovery order.** Players roam conversations freely.
When a player jumps to fact #7, the engine silently collapses only its
*ancestors* first (cascade depth ≈ 3–4 for 25 facts; still sub-microsecond).
Identical to the second-observer case: canon exists, this observer hasn't
read it yet. Consequence: **intimacy gates revelation, never collapse** —
deep facts may be silently pinned early. Accepted and documented; validator
warns when a shallow fact depends on a much deeper one.

**Depletion** is hypergeometric sampling (drawing without replacement) for
small places — integer decrements — and skipped above the threshold per the
correction above. Depletion makes worlds order-dependent: determinism is
per-world-state, place_state is part of the save, and that is intended.

## 4) Three-Layer Resolution (the world matrix)

Every resolution combines three layers, merged only at the moment of use:

1. **World matrix** — global factor set derived from active events (drought,
   war, storm) including magnitude tags (`threat:regional` vs
   `threat:personal`). Computed once per *event change*, cached. Never
   per-NPC, never per-conversation.
2. **Personal layer** — the entity's collapsed facts and persistent state.
3. **Session overlay** — the ephemeral product of the two, built at
   conversation start, used for collapses and renderer context, discarded
   after.

**The firewall invariant: transient effects live in the overlay; lasting
effects exist only via explicit collapse.** A storm making the farmer grumpy
today is overlay — gone tomorrow. The drought costing his harvest is a
collapsed fact. The world matrix cannot write to storage. Per-conversation
cost: one elementwise multiply of a precomputed vector. Rounds to free.

---

## 5) Intimacy as a Relationship System

Intimacy is divorced from generation depth (which becomes internal
bookkeeping). Three separated quantities: what **exists** (canon), what the
observer **knows** (observer record), what the relationship **is** (intimacy
score — a float on the observer record).

**Gains are event-weighted with context multipliers**, defined in JSON like
all content: five street conversations ≈ 5×1; the same five during a shared
adventure ≈ 5×3 plus a lump "we fought together" bonus. Shared experience
outweighs interrogation volume.

**Two gates on revelation, not one.** Threshold gate: is the relationship
deep enough for this topic at all? Rate gate: a per-conversation disclosure
budget — one or two new reveals per encounter, then "enough about me."
Solves the empty-seat exploit (standing in front of an NPC for ten minutes
cannot drain him); makes return visits intrinsically valuable.

**Losses are core; time-decay is optional and host-supplied.** Event-driven
drops (caught lying −20, betrayed mid-adventure −50) are entries in the same
source table — cheap, universal, time-agnostic, and more believable than
calendars. Time decay, where wanted, is a host-pluggable policy
`decay(elapsed, intimacy)` evaluated **lazily on touch** via the stored
`last_interaction` timestamp: one subtraction at next interaction, zero
background cost. 5,000 met NPCs are 5,000 dormant rows, not 5,000 timers.
The engine stores timestamps in host-defined units and never knows what time
means (Stellaris centuries vs. Bannerlord's single year).

## 6) Traits, Skills, and Outliers

A 1–10 stat is a ten-value enum — it inherits the entire fact system free
(seeds, factors, order, persistence). Facts shape traits via ordinary
factors (`profession=guard → bravery skewed high`, `age=elder → strength
down, experience up`); direction stays facts→traits.

**Outliers are free — they are distribution tails.** If elders carry a 4%
chance of bravery 9–10, the one old guardsman growling "let them come" at
the barbarian scare simply exists, deterministically, waiting to be met.
Design law: rules shift and skew distributions, never floor-and-ceiling
them — zeroing a tail authors the memorable characters out of existence.
Validator warns on tail-killing rules.

**Combat is observation.** Skills collapse the first time an NPC fights —
watching him swing the axe is the collapse of his combat skills, exactly as
mentioning his kids collapsed `has_children`. Recruiting an untested
companion is genuinely recruiting an unknown: a feature designed-stat
systems cannot offer.

---

## 7) Profiles — the Sockets for Other Modules

The engine exposes **values, never behavior**. A profile is a JSON-defined,
named projection mapping facts/traits onto a flat parameter bag another
module consumes:

- `combat_profile`: aggression = f(courage, bravery); preferred_range =
  f(profession, skills); flee_threshold = f(courage, age)
- `routine_profile`, `merchant_profile`, dialogue tone parameters (the
  renderer was using a profile all along)

Formulas live in JSON, authored per game; the engine evaluates and serves.
The combat AI calls `get_profile(entity, "combat_profile")` and never sees
raw facts; the engine never learns what combat is.

Boundaries: **equipment is host state, not engine state** — "master axeman
currently holding a skillet" is the combat module combining the engine's
*person* with the host's *inventory*. And **profile pulls are observation
events**: requesting a combat profile collapses the skills it reads, through
the same ancestor cascade. One pipeline, not two.

---

## 8) Simulation Hosts — Lazy History and the Active Set

For player-driven games the frozen-world law holds as-is. For simulation
hosts (4X, sandbox), two extensions:

**Lazy history.** Returning to an NPC after elapsed time collapses her
*recent past* at the moment of observation: given elapsed time, global
events that occurred (world matrix history), and existing facts, roll what
happened — shop lost, child born, joined the militia. Offscreen life is a
distribution sampled on touch. She appears to have lived continuously, and
no one can prove otherwise.

**The active set.** The host nominates "hot" entities (on screen, in party,
plot-critical) for per-tick action resolution; everyone else stays
frozen-with-lazy-history. Behavioral LOD — how every large sim works
internally — as a host-side choice, not engine machinery. Engine exposes
`resolve_actions(entity)` and `collapse_elapsed_history(entity, from, to)`;
the host decides who gets which and how often.

Softened invariant: no per-entity computation outside an interaction **or
explicit host nomination**. Cost scales with attention — the player's or
the host's — never with population.

## 9) Dialogue Rendering and the Passive AI

**The AI is a renderer, never a simulator.** No open text box; players pick
from options. Templates serve strangers; a small local model (1–3B
quantized, CPU-viable, no tokens, no internet) serves confidants. The
escalation gradient means inference cost scales with emotional investment,
not world size. Generated lines are cached as canon phrasings — consistent
voice for free, and most inference happens once, ever.

**Passive by construction, not instruction.** The prompt contract: here are
the facts (only those at or below the current revelation gate — the model
physically cannot overshare), intimacy as tone parameter, magnitude tags,
last exchange; phrase one line; nothing else. Never imply freedom — a small
model offered a door leaves the map entirely, and a rejection loop is a
failed box.

**Contradiction is solvable; freedom is not.** Pipeline: generate → extract
claims → validate against canon and population math → display. Pre-display
violation: silent regenerate. Post-display: in-character repair ("forgive
me, I misspoke") — which reads as human, not broken.

**Lies are engine-authored.** A deception rule layer distorts facts *before*
rendering (raiders:3 → "a horde") under stakes conditions; the AI phrases
the distortion believing it true. Truth and lie are both stored; every lie
is a logged, motivated, rare, discoverable event — confrontation is
queryable game state. Calibration is a player-facing contract, taught in a
tutorial beat (someone lies, gets caught): NPCs are mostly honest, sometimes
motivated to distort, and lies leave seams. 70% liars would make speech
worthless as a mechanic.

**Implicature is managed, not solved.** Players infer scale from word choice
("they're scary" vs "I'm afraid"). Mitigations: magnitude tags in the world
matrix flow to the prompt; and the design rule — *players may infer from
speech, but the game never confirms an inference that was not a collapsed
fact*. Unreliable narrators are diegetic; a frightened farmer would
exaggerate. The medieval setting self-masks low-grade generation: stilted
phrasing from a small model is characterization in a 12th-century mouth.

## 10) Guardrails — Problems → Solutions

| # | Problem | Solution |
|---|---------|----------|
| 1 | Rerolls create contradictions | Deterministic seeds `hash(world_seed, place_id, entity_id, fact_key)`; canon immutable |
| 2 | Hidden dependency-order bugs | Explicit collapse_order = DAG topological order; validator rejects against-order rules |
| 3 | P(A\|B) and P(B\|A) authored independently disagree | One canonical direction per dependency; validator-enforced |
| 4 | Forced discovery order feels railroaded | Silent ancestor cascade: collapse order internal, discovery order free |
| 5 | Early silent collapse pins deep facts before late context | Accepted: intimacy gates revelation, not collapse; validator warns shallow→deep dependencies |
| 6 | Per-interaction "update everything" cost | Lazy ancestral sampling: uncollapsed facts have no state; zero cost between collapses |
| 7 | World events force recalculating all met NPCs | Three-layer model: cached world matrix × personal layer → ephemeral session overlay; nothing written without collapse |
| 8 | 9 mayors in a 100-person town | Role bindings + hypergeometric depletion in place_state; skip threshold via finite population correction |
| 9 | Depletion breaks pure seed determinism | Documented: determinism is per-world-state; place_state is saved; order-dependence intended |
| 10 | Thousands of background trackers (decay, budgets) | No trackers exist: timestamps + lazy evaluation on touch; dormant rows are free |
| 11 | Time semantics vary wildly across games | Engine never defines time: host-unit timestamps, pluggable decay policy, event-driven losses as core |
| 12 | Empty-seat exploit (drain NPC in one sitting) | Dual gate: intimacy threshold + per-conversation disclosure budget |
| 13 | Speech referencing uncollapsed facts | Hard rule: speech references only collapsed facts or facts it collapses in-utterance |
| 14 | AI invents facts | Permanently passive renderer; prompt never implies freedom |
| 15 | AI contradicts canon | Claim-guard pipeline; silent regen or in-character repair |
| 16 | Implicature misleads players | Magnitude tags + "game never confirms non-canon inferences"; unreliable narrators diegetic |
| 17 | Lying as emergent AI noise | Engine-authored deception layer; truth+lie stored; rare, motivated, discoverable |
| 18 | Over-lying kills speech as a mechanic | Calibration contract, taught in tutorial |
| 19 | API costs / online dependency | Local small model; tight box makes small sufficient; template fallback always |
| 20 | Rules flooring/ceiling trait tails | Validator warns: tail-killing rules delete memorable outliers |
| 21 | Engine learning game concepts (combat, items) | Profile sockets: JSON projections, flat parameter bags; equipment stays host-side |
| 22 | Sims need a living world | Lazy history collapse + host-nominated active set (behavioral LOD) |

## 11) Invariants

1. A collapsed fact is never rerolled or overwritten.
2. Speech references only collapsed facts or facts collapsed in-utterance.
3. Canon lives on entities and places; knowledge lives on observer records;
   relationship is a score on observer records.
4. Intimacy gates revelation, never collapse.
5. Unique roles bind to one entity, place-wide, forever.
6. Transient effects live in the session overlay; only explicit collapse
   writes to storage. The world matrix cannot write.
7. The AI never originates facts; it renders what the engine hands it,
   including engine-authored lies it believes true. Every lie has a stored
   truth and a producing rule.
8. The engine exposes values (profiles), never behavior; it knows no time
   units, no items, no combat.
9. No per-entity computation outside an interaction or explicit host
   nomination.
10. Invalid content fails loudly at validation, never silently at runtime.
11. Cost scales with attention, never with the size of the implied world.

## 12) Data Model (key objects)

**Entity state** — collapsed_facts, collapsed_at (internal generation
tracker, no longer player-facing), metadata/seed_version.

**Place definition** (read-only content) — fact_groups, intimacy_map,
collapse_order, facts (enum + 1–10 trait enums), rules (factors + deception
rules), actions, profiles, intimacy_sources.

**Place state** (mutable, saved) —
```json
{
  "place_id": "town_hall",
  "population_total": 100,
  "depletion_tracking": true,
  "role_pools": { "artist": { "initial": 5, "remaining": 4 },
                  "mayor":  { "initial": 1, "remaining": 0 } },
  "role_bindings": { "mayor": "npc_000007" }
}
```

**Observer record** —
```json
{
  "entity_id": "npc_000123",
  "observer_id": "player_1",
  "intimacy": 34.5,
  "known_facts": ["gender", "profession", "has_children"],
  "last_interaction": 125900,
  "reveals_this_encounter": 1
}
```

**Intimacy source table** (content) —
```json
{
  "conversation": 1,
  "shared_adventure_bonus": 10,
  "adventure_context_multiplier": 3,
  "caught_lying_to_npc": -20,
  "betrayal": -50
}
```

**Profile definition** (content) —
```json
{
  "combat_profile": {
    "aggression":      { "formula": "0.6*courage + 0.4*bravery" },
    "flee_threshold":  { "formula": "10 - courage - 0.5*age_band_rank" },
    "preferred_range": { "map": { "guard": "melee", "hunter": "ranged" },
                         "from": "profession" }
  }
}
```

**Deception rule** (content) —
```json
{
  "id": "farmer_exaggerates_raiders",
  "type": "deception",
  "target": "fact:raider_threat",
  "when": { "all": [ { "fact": "profession", "equals": "farmer" },
                     { "event": "raids", "active": true },
                     { "stake": "personal_loss", "present": true } ] },
  "effect": { "distort": { "from": "minor", "to": "severe" },
              "tell_chance": 0.3 }
}
```

## 13) Worked Examples

**The blacksmith's children.** Player (intimacy 2, budget remaining) asks
about family. `has_children` is uncollapsed; parents `age_band`,
`marital_status` silently cascade-collapse first. Drought is active, so the
session overlay (world matrix × personal layer) skews the conversation
toward hardship topics. `has_children` collapses to `yes` *in the utterance*
"my little ones go hungry since the drought." Renderer receives facts +
`threat:regional` + intimacy 34 → one warm, worried line. Budget decrements.
Nothing else in the world computed anything.

**The defiant guardsman.** Global event `barbarian_raids` enters the world
matrix; 500 met NPCs cost zero. Each NPC talked to during the scare gets a
session overlay skewing fear topics. One elder collapsed years ago (in play
time) with bravery 9 — a 4% tail. His overlay + trait profile flips the
renderer tone parameter to defiant: "let them come — I want some action."
No special case exists anywhere in the content.

**The untested recruit.** Player recruits a laborer. Combat skills are
uncollapsed — he is genuinely an unknown. First skirmish, the combat module
calls `get_profile(entity, "combat_profile")`; the pull cascades collapse
of strength, axe_skill, courage. He turns out to be the farmhand with
sword_skill 8 — a tail outlier. From now on, that is canon, in every save,
forever.

**The mayor twice.** Player meets a stranger on the street; conversation
collapses `role: mayor`, claiming the binding `mayor → npc_000007` and
zeroing the role pool. Next day at the mayor's office, role resolution
returns the bound entity — same face, same facts, remembers yesterday.

## 14) Implementation & Testing Stages

### V1 — Deterministic Collapse Core
Build: validator → rule engine → trait resolver → action resolver → one
example place. Enum facts (incl. 1–10 traits), factors, DAG order with
silent ancestor cascade, deterministic seeds, abstract persistence, single
place JSON.
**Test:** golden-seed suites (same inputs → identical collapses across
runs/platforms); property tests (canon never overwritten; cascade collapses
exactly the ancestor set); validator fixtures (cycles, against-order rules,
zero-distributions, set_weights collisions, tail-killing rules all caught);
statistical tests (10k collapses per fact converge to authored
distributions, χ² check).

### V2 — Stateful World
Place state (depletion, role pools/bindings, skip threshold), observer
records (canon/knowledge split, intimacy score, disclosure budget, intimacy
source table, lazy decay hook), met-lists with small-world queries,
three-layer resolution (cached world matrix, session overlay,
write-through-only-via-collapse), speech topics as collapse requests with
template rendering.
**Test:** urn exhaustion (100-person town: roles deplete exactly, never
oversubscribe; mayor binds once); two-observer test (second observer rerolls
nothing); budget/threshold gating; overlay leak test (transient factors
never appear in storage); save/load round-trips of place_state + observer
records.

### V3 — Constrained Rendering
`dialogue_renderer` interface (template / local-LLM / scripted — host's
choice; core stays AI-agnostic), passive prompt contract, claim-guard
pipeline with regen + in-character repair, line cache as canon phrasings,
escalation gradient, template fallback, engine-side deception layer with
truth+lie logging.
**Test:** red-team the box (adversarial contexts trying to elicit invented
facts; measure claim-guard catch rate and regen budget); cache-consistency
(same context → same cached line); fallback drill (model unavailable →
seamless template tier); deception audit (every distorted utterance has
stored truth + rule id; tell_chance statistics match content).

### V4 — Social & Simulation Dynamics
Time-decay policies (host-supplied, lazily evaluated), lazy history
collapse, host-nominated active set with per-tick action resolution, gossip
propagation across met-lists, relationship reasoning, households —
structure already reserved, logic lands here. Active-AI collapse proposals
remain gated on model reliability; until then, permanently passive.
**Test:** lazy-history equivalence (NPC touched after N elapsed units lands
in statistically correct state vs. brute-force continuous sim baseline);
active-set scaling (frame cost linear in hot-set size, flat in total
population); decay correctness across wildly different host time scales.

## 15) Code Skeleton

```
living_npcs/
  core/
    rng.py                # stable seed: hash(world, place, entity, fact)
    validator.py          # DAG/direction checks, tail warnings, fail-loud
    rule_engine.py        # factor evaluation, priority, clamping
    trait_resolver.py     # intimacy gating, ancestor cascade, collapse
    action_resolver.py    # base weights -> factors -> weights/sample
    overlay.py            # world matrix cache + session overlay assembly
  world/
    place_state.py        # depletion, role pools/bindings, skip threshold
    observer.py           # records: intimacy, known_facts, budget, timestamps
    met_list.py           # reverse index + small-world queries
    speech_topics.py      # topic = collapse request + template binding
    history.py            # lazy elapsed-history collapse          (V4)
    active_set.py         # host-nominated hot entities            (V4)
  dialogue/
    renderer_iface.py     # render(context) -> line
    template_renderer.py  # always-available tier
    llm_renderer.py       # local model adapter, passive contract
    claim_guard.py        # extract -> validate -> regen/repair
    line_cache.py         # generated lines persisted as canon phrasing
    deception.py          # engine-side distortion; truth+lie logging
  profiles/
    profile_engine.py     # JSON projections -> flat parameter bags
  interfaces/
    persistence.py        # abstract: entity, place_state, observer, lines
    config_provider.py    # place definitions from host
    time_policy.py        # optional host decay(elapsed, intimacy)
  schemas/  examples/
```

**Core loop:**
```python
def collapse_fact(entity, fact_key, place, place_state, observer, ctx):
    if fact_key in entity.collapsed_facts:
        return entity.collapsed_facts[fact_key]        # canon immutable
    for parent in dag_ancestors(fact_key, place):       # silent cascade
        collapse_fact(entity, parent, place, place_state, observer, ctx)
    w = base_weights(place, fact_key)
    w = rule_engine.apply(w, fact_key, entity, overlay(ctx))  # factors
    w = place_state.apply_depletion(w, fact_key)
    value = weighted_pick(w, stable_seed(world, place.id, entity.id, fact_key))
    entity.collapsed_facts[fact_key] = value
    place_state.consume(fact_key, value)
    return value
```

## 16) Why It Stays Lean — the One-Paragraph Proof

Uncollapsed facts have no state, so there is nothing to update between
interactions. World events change one cached global vector, not N NPCs.
Decay and budgets are timestamps evaluated on touch, not timers. Depletion
is integer decrements in small places and provably skippable in large ones.
A full NPC collapse is a few hundred multiplications; a conversation is one
vector multiply plus at most a short ancestor cascade; inference is paid
only for emotionally invested relationships and cached forever after.
Every cost in the system is attached to a moment of attention — and
attention is the one resource that does not scale with world size.
